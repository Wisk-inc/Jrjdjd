What Automa is

Automa is an Android app from CorX Labs, made in Jamaica, that runs a whole company of AI agents for you. You create an organization with a goal, hire agents into an org chart, give them work in plain words, and watch them plan it, do it and check in when they need you. Every task traces back to the organization’s goal, hires, budgets and plans wait for your approval, and every token is counted against a budget with a hard stop.

The agents work on an Automa server you control: your computer, a cloud machine, or the phone itself inside the free Termux app. Nothing sits in the middle — no Automa cloud account, no ads, no tracking. And it runs on free AI models with no key, no account and no card, including Moan, Automa’s own Jamaican model that talks Patois.

What Automa does, in one minute

  • Hire AI agents with a role, a title, a manager and a model — a whole team in one tap from the teams catalog, or one agent at a time.
  • Chat with them like teammates: replies stream in, their thinking shows in a panel, they search the web and cite sources, read your attachments, draw pictures, and make real Excel, PowerPoint and Word files.
  • Give them tasks. Every task has one owner, a plan, a history and a cost. Agents ask you questions right in the task when they need a decision.
  • Approve what matters. Hires, budgets, plans and anything an agent gets stuck on wait for your yes in the Inbox. Agents never spend or hire on their own.
  • Build apps on the phone. App Studio writes, runs, previews and shares apps in 69 languages, with an AI pair or an AI team, and undo for every change.
  • Use free models. Twenty-plus chat models and two image models that need no key — including Moan, which talks Patois and knows Jamaica’s news, law and government.
  • Bring your own models too: Claude, GPT, Gemini, DeepSeek, Grok, Kimi, Cursor, Codex, Gemini CLI, OpenCode and any OpenAI-compatible endpoint.
  • Run coding terminals (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI) on the device, and take apps apart with App Inspector.
  • Count every token. Costs by agent, by task and by month, hard budget stops, and what the free work would have cost at paid prices.
  • Keep it yours. Organizations, chats and files live on your own server, which can be the phone in your hand.

Get started

  1. Install the APK — see Download and install.
  2. Connect. Enter a server address such as http://192.168.1.20:3100, or tap Run Automa on this phone and the app sets up a server inside Termux, with a progress bar for each step. A computer can also be linked over a USB cable or Wireless debugging.
  3. Create an organization with a name and a goal.
  4. Hire your first agent. Pick a name, a role and a model, or tap Skip for now: start on the free models.
  5. Say what you want done in Chats. The agent plans, creates tasks and asks when it needs you.
  6. Take the tour. One card per section, with Skip at every step; run it again any time from your profile.

Getting around

A bottom tab bar — Home, Chats, +, Tasks, Files — stays in thumb reach, a sidebar holds every section and the organization switcher, the bell opens the Inbox, and your avatar opens the account sheet. Long-press the app icon for shortcuts: New chat, New task, App Studio, Change server, Files. Every screen is edge-to-edge, keyboard-aware, and works in portrait and landscape on phones, tablets, foldables and Chromebooks. Destructive actions come with Undo, not “are you sure?” dialogs.

Home is your day at a glance: what needs you, who is working right now, what the free models saved, and live run activity.

Chats with your agents

Talk to an agent like a teammate. Replies stream token by token; the model’s thinking streams into its own panel and folds away when the answer starts. Tables wider than the screen scroll sideways. Source links show the site’s favicon. When a reply fails, the chat says why in plain words, and you can switch model and send again.

Pictures. Ask any free chat model for a picture and its paired image model draws it. Attach a photo and say what to change, and the model redraws it. Tap a picture to see it full size or Download it to the phone; Draw again asks for a fresh take.

The model switcher in the chat header lists every provider with its key status and the maker’s logo, a Free filter, thinking depth (Auto, Quick, Balanced, Deep, Deeper, Max), and switches for what a free agent may do: web search & reading, files & commands, teammates & tasks, connected apps. A paired image model gives a chat model the power to draw; a small “Bonded” mark shows the two makers’ logos together.

Messages. Copy, retry, delete and thumbs up or down on the newest reply; press and hold any other message for the same menu. Attach photos, screenshots, PDFs or any file with +. Unsent drafts survive leaving the chat. ⋯ on a chat pins, moves or deletes it.

Slash commands: /plan (plan first; nothing runs until you say so), /ask (answers only, no changes), /auto, /model, /think deep, /context, /stop, /clear, /skills, /help, /new.

Notifications. When an agent finishes a reply, or an App Studio job ends, while you are outside the app, a notification says so. Tap it to jump straight to that chat, task or project.

Pick a free model in the switcher and the agent runs on it; your other models stay where they are. Every model stays listed and says how it is doing — ready, busy, at today’s free limit and when it is back, or closed by its provider. Nothing is hidden.

What a free agent can do

Search the web across many results, read pages, crawl a site and download files; read, write, edit and search files and run commands in its workspace; make real Excel spreadsheets with formulas, PowerPoint decks with charts and pictures and Word documents, and read the spreadsheets, documents, decks, PDFs and CSVs you attach; draw with its paired image model; message teammates, hand off work, and create and update tasks; and request the connected apps it needs. It knows its role, its teammates and your name, and it keeps going through busy providers and dropped connections without ever switching models behind your back.

The Que engine sits under every free-model call: up to ten calls at once, at most two at a time on any one model, a pool of app IDs that grows on rate limits, retries on busy answers, a five-minute answer cache, conversations and batches. Many questions at once all get answered instead of some failing.

Tasks, approvals and the Inbox

Everything the agents are doing, each one a thread you can read, steer and approve. A task has one assignee, a parent and sub-tasks, blockers, comments with @mentions, a plan document, attachments and work products, labels, a priority and a status — backlog, to do, in progress, in review, blocked, done. The task page shows the live run with the agent’s thinking and tools, its cost and its full history. An agent can ask you a question with options right in the task; pick one and it carries on. When it finishes, it asks you to approve the result: approve to mark the task done, or send it back with what to change.

Inbox and approvals. Decisions waiting on you: hires, budgets, plans, join requests, failed runs and anything an agent got stuck on. Approve, decline or retry from the phone. The bell shows the count and clears when tapped. The Decisions page keeps the record of every automated and governed decision.

Agents and the org chart

Each agent has a role and title, an engine and model with the maker’s logo, a system prompt and instructions, a budget, skills, tools and a manager. The agent page shows its live runs, logs, progress, instructions and tools. Pause, change or offboard an agent any time. New agent is a short wizard; the teams catalog installs a whole team — a CEO with executives and engineers — in one go.

Engines: Claude Code · Anthropic API · Codex · Cursor (local and cloud) · Gemini CLI · Grok · Hermes · Kimi · OpenCode · OpenClaw gateway · Pi · Free models · metered API engines · a REST or webhook engine for an agent you host elsewhere. On a phone, a Claude agent answers through the Anthropic API with Claude Code’s own tools running in Termux.

Projects, goals and routines

Projects group tasks under one goal and keep their own workspace. Goals are the top of the task tree, the “why” every task hangs from. Routines run work on a schedule — a daily summary, a weekly review — and each run is a task you can read. Pipelines chain stages of work, cases collect related work into one file, and workspaces show where an agent’s run happens, with its live service ports.

Artifacts and your phone’s files

Artifacts are every file the agents produced, grouped by task, with a viewer for text, images and code. Downloads go to Downloads/Automa.

Files shares one folder on the phone with your agents. With auto-send on, a file an agent asks for is sent without asking; otherwise the request waits for you to pick a file or decline. Agents can send files back. Use Share to Automa from any app’s share sheet and the file waits on the Files page until you confirm.

Auto-send removes you from the loop — that is the feature and the risk. With it off, every file leaves the phone because you tapped Send. With it on, anything inside the shared folder can leave without a decision at the moment it happens. Share a folder that exists for this, not your whole Documents tree.

Skills and connectors

Skills are what agents know how to do. Install them from the catalog or build one in Skill Studio; a new skill is available from the agent’s next run. Connectors are the apps agents can reach: GitHub, Slack, Gmail, Notion and more. You choose which agents get access, which accounts they use and which actions they may take — three choices, not one switch. Connecting an app stays inside Automa.

App Studio: build apps from your phone

Build, run and share apps with an AI pair, or a whole AI team, that writes the code and fixes what breaks. No key needed: the free models work in the AI panel and for the editor’s code suggestions.

  • 69 languages in 11 groups, with quick starts for a website, Python, a full-stack app, React, Flask, Go, Rust, a mobile app and TypeScript.
  • Describe your app in plain words and the assistant builds it from the first message, or clone from GitHub by pasting a link.
  • On a phone each part is a tab: Files, Code, Console, Preview, AI, Stats, Team. The editor saves on its own and suggests code as you type.
  • Preview runs the app inside Automa, sandboxed. One tap opens a public link anyone can open. Missing toolchains install on the device with progress.

The AI assistant reads and searches the files, writes and edits code, runs commands and tests, starts and stops the app, reads the console, searches the web, and keeps going until the job is done. It sees the page like you do: it opens the running page at phone or desktop size, reads what is on screen, the console errors and the layout trouble, fixes what it found and looks again.

AI teams. Pick teammates in the model sheet and the lead model hands them work: several at once, each with a role and the files it owns, talking to each other while they work. The Stats tab shows a running clock, steps, files changed and every model at work with live tokens, then the totals and what the free work would have cost.

Undo and safety. Undo any change the assistant made, step by step. Checkpoints are saved before every AI message, Stop is instant, and nothing is ever written outside the project folder. Build together: invite people with a join code or link, for an hour up to no limit. Guests see your code as you type, chat, send files and suggest changes you review as a diff; they can’t save files or reach the rest of Automa.

Terminals and App Inspector

Terminals give you a real coding terminal on the device: Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, OpenCode and others in their full terminal UI, on a Debian layer the page sets up for you. Wire a terminal to an agent so it works with that agent’s key, pick a model per tool, or open the free models chat with nothing to install.

App Inspector takes an app on your phone, or any APK, apart: it decodes the manifest, resources and code, shows the code as Java, lets you search, edit and ask Claude to explain any file, and rebuilds a signed APK to your Downloads. It is for apps you made or are allowed to analyze.

Costs, budgets and usage limits

Every token is counted: agent runs, chats, App Studio replies and code suggestions land on Costs by agent, by task and by month. The organization and each agent have a budget, and a hard stop pauses spending at the limit. The usage limits card shows how much of the free models’ monthly allowance is used and when it renews, each free model’s daily limit with a countdown, and Claude’s session and weekly limits when Claude Code is signed in. The free models card on Home and Costs shows what the free work would have cost at paid prices.

Search, audit and the timeline

Search finds tasks, comments, plan documents, artifacts, agents and projects in one place. Audit holds the log of everything that happened, who did it and when, with the runs, costs and budgets behind it. The work timeline shows every agent’s runs side by side.

Settings and your profile

Organization settings: general (name, goal, logo, budget), members and access (invites, roles, join requests), API keys — one place for the keys agents, chats and App Studio run on, with a monthly spending limit — secrets, adapters, environments, plugins and Experimental switches. The account sheet shows your profile, your API balance, the tour, the dark theme and sign-out, and About Automa keeps the full version history.

How it is wired

Three parts, and it matters which one is where. The phone holds the app and one shared folder — the whole board UI ships inside the APK, so the layout is the same whatever server it talks to. The server holds the agents, their tasks and their routines, and does the work. The connections are how the server reaches services that are not yours to host. Solid lines carry work outward; dashed lines are the three things that come back to you.

Running Automa on the phone

No computer needed. Install Termux from F-Droid, tap Run Automa on this phone in the app, and paste the command Termux is given. The installer sets up Node.js and PostgreSQL, the server and an automa command, then starts Automa on 127.0.0.1:3100, locked to the app. The first install downloads a few hundred packages and takes 10 to 45 minutes; updates are much faster.

Automa commands in Termux
CommandWhat it does
automa runRun in the foreground, restarting the server if it stops
automa start · stop · statusRun in the background, stop it, check it
automa updateUpdate the server; it backs up the database first
automa backup · restoreSave a database backup now, or put one back
automa linkHand the app key to the Automa app again
automa adb linkLet agents drive the phone over adb: tap, type, screenshot

The app starts Automa for you when it isn’t running and shows Automa is starting over your page while the server comes up. Backups are made every hour while Automa runs and before every update, and a restore never loses the database. Set Termux’s battery use to Unrestricted; with Termux:Boot, Automa starts when the phone boots.

Security and privacy

  • Your data stays with you. Organizations, tasks, chats and files live on your own Automa server. The app talks to the server you connect it to, and to Google only for sign-in and push notifications when those are set up. Free-model requests go from your server to the free AI service Automa uses.
  • Locked to the app. On a phone, Automa answers only the Automa app, with a key the installer makes; the app proves the server holds that key before ever sending it, and never sends it to another program on the phone.
  • Agents see only what they should. Agents and terminals never see the server’s database password or sign-in secret, every organization’s data is kept separate, and keys saved in App Studio are encrypted on your server.
  • Sandboxed previews. App Studio previews can’t act as you or reach the rest of Automa, and free agents’ web tools never reach private or local addresses.
  • Governed by you. Hires, budgets and plans wait for your approval; budgets stop spending; invites can’t give more than the person who made them has.
  • Hard to tamper with. Release builds are signed and obfuscated, and signing keys never ship in the APK.

Limits, stated plainly

  • Android only. Android 8.0 or newer, on phones, tablets, foldables and Chromebooks. There is no iPhone build.
  • The AI needs internet. With the server on the phone, Automa itself keeps working offline; the AI features do not.
  • Free models have limits. Each has a daily limit, they share a monthly allowance, and a provider can be busy or close a model. Automa shows which, and when it is back.
  • Some engines can’t run on a phone. Codex agents can’t, and say so; other runtimes must be installed in Termux. Claude Code’s desktop engine doesn’t run in Termux, so on a phone Claude agents use the Anthropic API with Claude Code’s tools.
  • Moan’s index is Jamaican. For the rest of the Caribbean, agents research the web instead.
  • App Inspector is for apps you made or are authorized to analyze.

Questions people ask

What is Automa?

Automa is an Android app, made in Jamaica by Nathan at CorX Labs, that runs a whole company of AI agents for you. You hire agents, give them roles, chat with them like teammates, hand them tasks, let them build and run apps, draw pictures, search the web and work with your files — and you approve anything that matters. The agents work on an Automa server you control: your computer, a cloud machine, or the phone itself inside the free Termux app.

Do I need an API key or a credit card?

No. Pick a free model and start: Automa’s free models need no key, no account and no card. Add keys later under Settings → API keys if you want Claude, GPT, Gemini or another paid model, and set a monthly spending limit so nothing overspends.

Do I need a computer?

No. Tap Run Automa on this phone and the server runs inside Termux on the phone. A computer, a virtual machine or a cloud machine works too, and is what you want if the agents should keep working while the phone sleeps.

Is there an AI that speaks Jamaican Patois?

Yes. Moan is Automa’s own Jamaican model, free with no key. It answers in Patois first and in Standard Jamaican English for formal documents and code, carries a primer on Jamaica’s history, government, courts, statutes, economy and culture, and keeps a living index of Jamaican sources — the Gleaner, the Observer, JIS, the Office of the Prime Minister, Parliament, the Bank of Jamaica and the Laws of Jamaica — so its replies cite source, date and link.

Can I use Automa elsewhere in the Caribbean?

Automa itself is not tied to Jamaica: you install the APK yourself and the agents run on a server you control. Free agents search and read the web with sources, so research about Trinidad and Tobago, Barbados, Guyana, the Bahamas or anywhere else works the same way. Moan’s built-in knowledge index is the Jamaica-specific part.

Which AI models does Automa work with?

Twenty-plus free chat models and two free image models with no key — including Moan, Nemotron 3 Ultra, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Gemma 4 and Qwen 3.8, plus SDXL Lightning and SDXL Base for pictures. With your own keys or subscriptions it runs Claude, GPT, Gemini, DeepSeek, Grok and Kimi, and engines such as Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini CLI, OpenCode and any OpenAI-compatible endpoint.

Can Automa make pictures, spreadsheets and documents?

Yes. A free chat model paired with an image model draws pictures and logos, and redraws an attached photo with the change you ask for. Free agents also make real Excel spreadsheets with formulas, PowerPoint decks with charts and pictures, and Word documents, and read the files you attach.

Can I build apps on my phone with Automa?

Yes, in App Studio: 69 languages, an AI pair or a whole AI team that writes the code and fixes what breaks, a sandboxed preview, a public link in one tap, undo for every AI change, and friends invited by code or link. It works on the free models with no key.

What happens offline?

With the server on the phone, Automa itself keeps working offline. The AI features need internet. When the phone loses the network, a sheet says so and counts down to the next try, and what you typed is kept.

Is there an account, and where is my data?

There is no Automa cloud account. Your organizations, tasks, chats and files live on your own Automa server, and sign-in exists only on that server, when you turn it on. Free-model requests go from your server to the free AI service Automa uses.

Can other people use my organization?

Yes. Invite members with roles; an invite can’t give more than the person who made it has. App Studio projects can be shared with friends by join code or link.

Is there an iPhone version?

No. Automa is for Android 8.0 or newer, on phones, tablets, foldables and Chromebooks.

How do I download Automa?

Downloading Automa is pending on APKPure. Version 1.9.7 has been submitted to APKPure and is waiting on its review; once the review is done, it will be released there to download. Every release from 1.8.2 on installs over the previous one and keeps your data.

Who made Automa?

Nathan, the 14-year-old founder of CorX Labs in Jamaica, who also built the CorX language models and NatureV1. Automa builds on an MIT-licensed open-source control plane for AI agents.
