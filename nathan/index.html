Nathan Lyttle

Fourteen, from Jamaica, and the founder of CorX Labs. He has built five open-source AI models and an Android app on his own, on a single GPU — and published each one with its limits written down.

Nathan Lyttle at a glance

Nathan Lyttle is a 14-year-old developer from Jamaica and the founder of CorX Labs, an independent AI research lab. Working alone, he has built CorX1.5, a language model trained from random weights; CorX3.8-27B, Jamaica’s first large open-weight language model; NatureV1, an AI weather and hurricane model; TriStream-SVS and HELIX; and Automa, an Android app for running AI agents. Every model is open source under Apache 2.0.

How he got here

  1. At twelve, he wired up a chatbot on top of Gemini through its API and shaped how it behaved. It worked, but it was somebody else’s model.
  2. At thirteen, he tried fine-tuning. Slow GPUs and long runs got the better of it, and he stopped — which is why the next attempt started from random weights.
  3. At fourteen, he wrote and trained CorX1.5 from scratch. Through 2026 came TriStream-SVS, CorX3.8-27B, HELIX, Automa and NatureV1.

The longer version, attempt by attempt, is on the developers page.

How he works

  • Alone, from Jamaica. Every model, the Automa app, the benchmark index and this website.
  • On one GPU. CorX1.5 was pretrained on about 3 billion tokens; NatureV1 trained in about a week.
  • In the open. Weights on Hugging Face, code on PyPI, every model under Apache 2.0.
  • With the limits written down. CorX3.8-27B is labelled a fine-tune. NatureV1’s page says its hurricane track errors are 2.5 to 4.5 times the National Hurricane Center’s, and that it is not for real decisions.

Questions

Who is Nathan Lyttle?

Nathan Lyttle is a 14-year-old developer from Jamaica and the founder of CorX Labs, an independent AI research lab. He is the only person who has worked on it: every model, the Automa app, the benchmark index and this website.

How old is Nathan?

Nathan is 14 (as of 2026). He started working with AI model APIs at twelve, tried fine-tuning at thirteen, and wrote his first model from scratch, CorX1.5, at fourteen.

What has Nathan built?

Five open-source AI models and one app: CorX1.5, a 158M language model trained from random weights; TriStream-SVS, a singing voice synthesis model; CorX3.8-27B, Jamaica’s first large open-weight language model; HELIX, a linear-scaling sequence architecture; NatureV1, an AI weather and hurricane research model; and Automa, an Android app for running a company of AI agents.

Did Nathan build his AI models from scratch?

CorX1.5 was written and trained from random weights — the architecture, the tokenizer and the training pipeline. TriStream-SVS, HELIX and NatureV1 are his own designs. CorX3.8-27B is a fine-tune of the open-weight Qwen3.8-27B base, and the lab says so plainly.

Does Nathan work alone?

Yes. CorX Labs is a one-person lab. The models, their training, the documentation, the Automa app and this website were all built by Nathan, on a single GPU.

Where is Nathan from?

Jamaica. CorX Labs is an independent AI research lab and research station in Jamaica, in the Caribbean.

How can I contact Nathan?

By email at lkk89002@gmail.com, or through the contact page. Media, event and business inquiries are welcome. CorX Labs also posts updates on X as @corxlabs.
